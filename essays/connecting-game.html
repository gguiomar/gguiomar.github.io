Click a controller to tilt it: ↑ glyphs, ← letters, → numbers. Shift-click tilts back, or use the arrow keys on a focused controller. Nobody tells you which cells a controller moves.

The clue

Every team received this strip of paper, written in the cipher, next to its controller.

Cipher key

Earned symbol by symbol during the retreat's earlier games. The arms of each glyph encode a hex digit.

How it worked

On the last night of the 2019 retreat, about two hundred researchers sat on a dance floor in front of this grid. Twenty-five wireless accelerometers were spread through the room. Each one could tilt three ways and drove four mirrored cells of the grid: one in each quadrant.

Nobody was told the controls or the goal. Each team only had the clue. Every controller tilted the same way: up for the cipher glyphs, left for the letters, right for the numbers. Because each one moved cells in all four quadrants, fixing a word in one corner changed the others, so the room had to agree. The game was solved when every controller under the sentence showed letters and every other one did not; then the grid disappeared and the sentence stayed.

This is a single-player version of the game. Original game by Gonçalo Guiomar (creative direction) and Isaque Sanches (game art and code), designed together; symbols by Pietro Vertechi and Diogo Matias, controllers by the Collective Motion Laboratory.
