01

Understanding reasoning dynamics in Large Language Models

+ CognitionInterpretabilityLLMs

Can you beat a language model? Try the task yourself.

Background

Current LLMs solve remarkably complex tasks, but how they do it is very hard to interpret. Minimal, psychophysics-inspired probabilistic-reasoning tasks let us compare humans and language models on exactly the same problems, and act as fine-grained probes of the latent computations behind reasoning (Guiomar & Torre et al., 2026). Mechanistic interpretability, however, still lacks reliable ways to connect this kind of time-extended behavior to the internal representations and circuits that generate it.

Thesis

Using our task suite, you will perform mechanistic interpretability on reasoning language models. You will record activations and attention patterns while models solve the tasks, identify the circuits that encode latent cognitive variables such as belief, uncertainty and memory, and test causally, with activation patching and ablations, how these circuits drive behavior, comparing the models against human data.

Main points
  • Running open reasoning models on minimal probabilistic-reasoning tasks and comparing their behavior with humans.
  • Finding and causally validating the circuits behind belief and uncertainty updates.
  • Formalizing mathematically how reasoning traces implement latent-state inference.
You'll gain

Modern interpretability and analysis workflows, large-scale activation datasets, and LLM inference and evaluation pipelines on HPC, at the interface of cognitive science, neuroscience and the mechanistic auditing of reasoning models.

Profile

Strong Python and PyTorch skills; familiarity with LLMs; interest in interpretability and cognitive science.

Reading
  • Guiomar & Torre et al. (2026). Reasoning under uncertainty in humans and language models.
  • Kriegeskorte & Kievit (2013). Representational geometry: integrating cognition, computation, and the brain.
  • Conmy et al. (2023). Towards Automated Circuit Discovery for Mechanistic Interpretability.
  • Jo et al. (2025). Causal Path Tracing in Transformers.
Supervisors

Dr. Gonçalo Guiomar, Elia Torre, Prof. Valerio Mante (ETH, UZH)

02

Symbolic model discovery using bifurcation theory

+ Dynamical systemsRNNsInterpretability
Background

Recurrent networks trained on simple stochastic tasks develop rich internal dynamics that are hard to read out from their parameters. In Torre et al. (2025), RNNs driven only by noise learn to emulate Hidden Markov Models: slow populations integrate the noise and a few near-threshold "kick" neurons trigger switches between states along a closed loop. Our follow-up work shows that these high-dimensional stochastic dynamics reduce to low-dimensional stochastic normal forms from bifurcation theory, the cusp and its unfoldings, so that a network of hundreds of neurons is described by a handful of interpretable equations. Following training checkpoint by checkpoint, bistability emerges through generic fold (saddle-node) bifurcations.

Thesis

You will build a pipeline for symbolic model discovery: identify the slow coordinates of trained networks, search over families of candidate stochastic differential equations constrained by normal-form theory, and validate the resulting models against the network's state densities, switching statistics and responses to perturbations.

Directions
  • Automating normal-form identification with sparse or symbolic regression and principled model selection.
  • Going beyond two states: multi-state, cyclic and fully connected HMMs, where higher-codimension normal forms are expected.
  • Characterizing the bifurcations networks cross during learning, and how architecture and regularization shape them.
  • Testing noise as a bifurcation parameter in circuits with broken symmetries.
You'll gain

A working knowledge of dynamical systems and bifurcation theory, stochastic differential equations, and mechanistic interpretability of recurrent networks.

Profile

Strong Python skills and experience training recurrent neural networks; a background in dynamical systems, physics or applied mathematics is a plus.

Reading
  • Torre et al. (2025). Mechanistic Interpretability of RNNs emulating Hidden Markov Models.
  • Kuznetsov (2004). Elements of Applied Bifurcation Theory.
  • Muratov, Vanden-Eijnden & E (2005). Self-induced stochastic resonance in excitable systems.
Supervisors

Elia Torre, Dr. Gonçalo Guiomar, Prof. Valerio Mante

03

Online Reinforcement Learning

+ RLWorld modelsDistributed computing
Background

World models let agents plan by imagining trajectories instead of interacting purely online, improving sample efficiency. Most planners roll their model out one step at a time, so errors compound and long horizons become expensive. Jumpy world models instead predict where a policy will end up over a random, geometrically distributed horizon, so a planner can compose a few subgoals that each cover many steps. In our ongoing work on online policy composition, the policy, the jumpy model and the planner are all learned together from scratch during interaction, and planning over policy occupancies improves goal-conditioned agents on long-horizon navigation and manipulation tasks.

Thesis

You will develop and evaluate online model-based RL with jumpy world models, combining algorithmic work with large-scale experiments across GPUs and nodes.

Directions
  • Scaling the number of composed subgoals during online training at matched compute.
  • Distributed online RL in JAX: parallel environments, planner sampling across GPUs and nodes, and actor–learner separation on the cluster.
  • Learning when to replan: adaptive commitment and planning horizons.
  • Comparing jumpy and one-step world models (e.g. TD-MPC2) at equal planning budget.
You'll gain

Deep experience with model-based and goal-conditioned RL, modern world-model architectures, JAX, and large-scale experiments on GPU clusters.

Profile

Proficiency in Python and JAX or PyTorch, and prior experience with at least one RL codebase.

Reading
  • Thakoor et al. (2022). Generalised Policy Improvement with Geometric Policy Composition.
  • Janner, Mordatch & Levine (2020). Gamma-Models: Generative Temporal Difference Learning for Infinite-Horizon Prediction.
  • Hansen, Su & Wang (2024). TD-MPC2: Scalable, Robust World Models for Continuous Control.
  • Aceituno et al. (2025). Temporal horizons in forecasting: a performance-learnability trade-off.
Supervisors

Dr. Gonçalo Guiomar (ETH, UZH), Dr. Pau Aceituno (UZH)

04

Recursive Vision Models

+ VisionRL
Background

Vision Transformers (ViTs) and related architectures achieve state-of-the-art performance but are often computationally expensive due to quadratic attention. Linear and efficient attention variants, as well as biologically inspired recurrent vision models, seek to reduce cost while retaining performance and robustness. Visual RL benchmarks show that compact visual backbones can significantly impact sample efficiency and generalization.

Thesis

You will validate a biologically inspired recurrent vision architecture on modern vision RL benchmarks. The focus is on linear-attention or recurrent designs that can handle sequences of visual observations in RL environments, and on understanding how architectural choices affect sample efficiency, robustness, and emergent visual reasoning.

You'll gain

Experience in efficient vision architectures, reinforcement learning, and the design of small models capable of non-trivial reasoning over visual inputs.

Profile

Comfortable using PyTorch and some familiarity with RL libraries. Interest in biologically inspired models and efficient architectures is important.

Reading
  • Choromanski et al. (2020). Rethinking Attention with Performers.
  • Han et al. (2023). FLatten Transformer: Vision Transformer using Focused Linear Attention.
  • Yao et al. (2024). Mobile-Friendly Linear Attention for Vision Transformers.
  • Xu et al. (2024). Improving Linear Attention in ViT with Quadratic Taylor Expansion.
  • Tao et al. (2023). Evaluating Vision Transformer Methods for Deep Reinforcement Learning Control Tasks.

05

Real-time scene understanding

+ Vision3D poseEdge computing
Background

Markerless 3D motion capture of several people at once usually needs a dedicated studio or offline processing. PCAT (Probabilistic Cross-View Assignment by Triangulation) reconstructs and tracks the 3D poses of multiple people in real time from a ring of synchronized, calibrated RGB cameras. A lightweight 2D pose detector runs on every view, PCAT probabilistically assigns detections across views by sampling and triangulating 3D pose proposals, and a causal tracker keeps identities consistent over time. The whole pipeline is written in JAX and turns twelve camera views into tracked 3D skeletons at close to the cameras' frame rate on a single GPU.

Thesis

You will take the system further along one of two axes: from a single GPU to a distributed, real-time sensing network, or from tracked skeletons to an understanding of what is happening in the scene.

Directions
  • Distributed edge inference on a cluster of embedded boards: 2D inference on each camera node, compact keypoints streamed to a fusion node; latency, synchronization and bandwidth.
  • Low-latency causal filtering (Kalman, fixed-lag smoothing) for smooth real-time tracking.
  • Accuracy benchmarks on public multi-view datasets, and online self-calibration of the camera network.
  • From skeletons to behavior: recognizing actions and interactions from tracked 3D poses.
You'll gain

Multi-view geometry and 3D pose estimation, real-time machine-learning systems in JAX, and deployment on embedded hardware.

Profile

Strong Python; background in computer vision or robotics; experience with JAX or PyTorch; interest in real-time systems.

Reading
  • Hartley & Zisserman (2004). Multiple View Geometry in Computer Vision.
  • Kalman (1960). A New Approach to Linear Filtering and Prediction Problems.
  • Rauch, Tung & Striebel (1965). Maximum likelihood estimates of linear dynamic systems.
Supervisors

Dr. Gonçalo Guiomar (ETH, UZH)
